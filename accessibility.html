Accessibility Statement

Last updated 3 September 2026. First published 15 January 2026.

Note. This statement is a starting template. The conformance claim below should be verified by an independent audit, and the statement reviewed by a lawyer, before this site is used commercially.

Our commitment

This website should be usable by everybody, including people using a screen reader, a keyboard only, a switch device, voice control, screen magnification, or a browser with text scaled up. Accessibility is the main reason this site looks the way it does, rather than an adjustment made to it afterwards.

Conformance

We aim to meet Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG) 2.1 at Level AA. We believe the site currently conforms, with the exceptions honestly listed under "Known limitations" below. This is a self-assessment. It has not been independently audited and we will say so plainly until it has been.

As an Ontario business we are also subject to the Accessibility for Ontarians with Disabilities Act and its Information and Communications Standards, which reference WCAG 2.0 Level AA. We are working to the newer 2.1 guidelines because they cover mobile and low vision better.

What we have done

Structure

  • Every page uses proper HTML landmarks: a banner header, a navigation region with a label, a main region, and a contentinfo footer.
  • Headings run in order without skipping levels. One h1 per page, describing what the page is.
  • A "Skip to content" link is the first thing in the tab order on every page.
  • Breadcrumbs on every page below the top level.
  • Lists are marked up as lists and tables as tables, with a caption and scoped header cells on every table.
  • Every page declares its language as English (Canada).

Keyboard

  • Everything on this site can be operated with a keyboard alone. There are no mouse-only controls, because there are no custom controls at all.
  • Focus is always visible: a 2px accent outline with a 3px offset, never removed and never set to none.
  • The mobile menu is a modal surface, so focus is kept inside it while it is open and returned to the button that opened it on close.
  • Focus order follows reading order, because the layout is a single column and the DOM order is the visual order.
  • There are no keyboard traps. Pressing Escape closes the mobile menu and returns focus to the button that opened it.
  • Tables that scroll horizontally are focusable and can be scrolled with the arrow keys.

Screen readers

  • Every image has alt text describing what is in the photograph, not the file name and not a keyword list. Purely decorative images would carry an empty alt attribute; there are none, because we do not use decorative images.
  • Form fields have real labels, associated by "for" and "id". Placeholder text is never used as a label.
  • Validation errors are written in text beneath the affected field, announced through a live region, and start with the word "Error" so that colour is never the only indicator.
  • Product filtering announces the new result count through a polite live region, so a screen reader user is told what changed.
  • Link text makes sense out of context. There is no "click here" and no "read more" on its own.

Vision and contrast

  • Body text is a near-black warm ink on warm paper, a contrast ratio of about 16:1 against a required minimum of 4.5:1.
  • Secondary text is about 7.6:1 and the lightest metadata about 5.2:1. The rust accent used for links, prices and primary buttons is about 6.8:1, and the error red about 6.3:1. Every one of them exceeds AA.
  • Links inside body copy are underlined, and the active navigation item is marked with weight as well as colour. Colour is never the only signal.
  • Text is real text, never an image of text.
  • The layout reflows to a single column at 320 pixels wide without horizontal scrolling, and survives text being enlarged to 200 per cent.
  • Typefaces are system stacks rather than downloaded webfonts, so they respect your operating system's font substitution settings, including any dyslexia-friendly font you have configured at the OS level, and no text is ever invisible while a font loads.
  • The site follows your system light or dark preference. Both themes are contrast-checked to the same standard; neither is an afterthought.

Motion

  • Motion is limited to reporting a state: a card lifting under the cursor, the mobile menu sliding in, a block settling as you scroll to it. Nothing loops, nothing moves on its own, and nothing moves while you are trying to read it.
  • There is no parallax, no carousel, no auto-playing anything, and no animation longer than about four tenths of a second.
  • If your device asks for reduced motion, all of it stops. Transitions and animations are reduced to zero, the scroll-reveal is skipped entirely rather than merely shortened, and hover transforms are removed. Content is never left hidden or displaced as a result.
  • The scroll-reveal is applied by JavaScript only. If the script does not run, or your browser lacks the relevant API, nothing is hidden in the first place.
  • The back-to-top button jumps rather than glides when reduced motion is requested.
  • There is no audio or video on this site, so there is nothing to caption and nothing that plays without being asked.

Touch and mobile

  • Interactive targets are at least 44 by 44 CSS pixels, which is both the WCAG 2.5.5 recommendation and the iOS Human Interface Guidelines minimum.
  • Form inputs are set at 16px so that iPhone Safari does not zoom the page when a field is focused.
  • The layout respects the notch and home indicator on modern iPhones through safe-area insets, so nothing sits under the system UI.
  • The site works in both portrait and landscape and does not lock orientation.
  • Nothing requires a gesture more complex than a single tap.

Known limitations

Every accessibility statement that claims perfection is wrong. Here is what we know is imperfect.

  • Product photographs are supplied by third-party photographers. We write the alt text ourselves, but we are describing somebody else's photograph. If a description is inaccurate, tell us and we will fix it the same day.
  • Long size tables are dense. The size guide tables carry a lot of numbers and are hard work in a screen reader even with proper headers. If you would like the measurements for a specific garment sent to you as plain text or read out on the phone, email or ring us and we will do it.
  • No independent audit yet. Everything above is our own testing. We intend to commission an audit and will publish the result here when we have.
  • No French translation. The site is English only at present, which is a barrier for some Canadian customers. Telephone and email support is available in French. A French version of the site is planned but not yet dated, and we will not pretend otherwise.
  • Advertisement slots. Advertisements served by third parties are outside our control and may not meet the same standard as the rest of the page. They are clearly labelled, they never sit above the main content, and they never appear on the legal pages or the error page.

Reporting a problem

If any part of this site does not work for you, tell us. We treat accessibility faults as urgent, ahead of feature work.

Email: access@plaintextsupply.ca
Telephone: +1 (416) 555-0148
Post: Accessibility, Plaintext Supply Co., 214 Sterling Road, Unit 6, Toronto, Ontario M6R 2B2, Canada

Tell us the page, what you were trying to do, and what happened. It helps if you can say what browser and assistive technology you are using, but do not worry if you cannot. We acknowledge within two business days and aim to fix within ten, and if a fix will take longer than that we will tell you why and offer a way around it in the meantime.

If you are not satisfied with our response, you may contact the Accessibility Directorate of Ontario, or the Canadian Human Rights Commission where federal jurisdiction applies.
