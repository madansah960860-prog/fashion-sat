Outerwear

Eight coats and jackets. Between them they cover the range a Canadian year actually demands, from a wet Vancouver October to a dry minus twenty in Winnipeg. We do not make a piece for every temperature; we make pieces that layer, so three garments cover eight months.

Looking after outerwear

Waxed cotton

Waxed cotton is not waterproof, it is water resistant, and the difference matters. It will keep you dry through a two-hour downpour and it will not keep you dry through a day of it. Do not wash it, ever. Water and detergent strip the wax and once it is stripped the cloth is just cotton. Sponge mud off with cold water and let it dry away from heat. Re-wax at the end of each winter: warm the tin, work the wax in with a cloth in small circles, then hang the jacket somewhere warm for a few hours so the wax draws into the weave. We sell no wax ourselves; any pure paraffin dressing works.

Wool

Wool cleans itself if you let it. A stiff clothes brush, used along the nap after each wearing, removes more dirt than dry cleaning does and does not degrade the fibre. Dry clean once a year at the outside. If the coat gets soaked, hang it on a wide wooden hanger away from a radiator and let it dry slowly; heat felts wool and felted wool never comes back.

Technical shells

The most common mistake is not washing a shell often enough. Body oil and dirt block the membrane and make a jacket feel like it is leaking when it is actually just saturated on the outside. Wash it warm with a technical detergent every ten to fifteen wearings, tumble it low afterwards, and re-proof when water stops beading on the shoulders.
