18 oz Canvas Tote

78 USD. In stock, one size, undyed natural.

Gallery

The fourth frame is a two-year-old bag beside a new one. We photograph it that way because that is what you are actually buying: a bag that will look like the older one within a year or so.

Specification

  • Fabric: 100% cotton canvas, 18 oz, undyed and unbleached. Woven in North Carolina, cut and sewn in Toronto.
  • Construction: Flat-felled base seam, so there is no raw edge inside to fray. Handles are 1.5 in cotton webbing, run down the full height of the bag on both faces rather than stitched to the rim, and bar-tacked at six points. One internal patch pocket, 7 by 6 in.
  • Fit: 16 in wide, 15 in tall, 5 in gusset. Handle drop 10.5 in, which clears a winter coat shoulder. Empty weight 480 g.
  • Care: Machine wash cold on its own, hang to dry. It will shrink about 3 per cent on the first wash and then stop. Do not bleach undyed canvas; it goes yellow rather than white.
  • Sizing: One size. Holds a 15 in laptop flat against the back face, or roughly two days of groceries, or a swim kit and a pair of boots.
  • Model: Not worn. Photographed on the studio floor at Sterling Road, unstyled and unlit apart from the window.
  • Load: Tested to 18 kg in-house. We do not claim a certified figure because we have not paid for a certified test.
  • Made in: Toronto, Ontario.

Why 18 oz

Most tote bags are 8 to 12 oz canvas. That is light, cheap and folds nicely, and it also collapses the moment you put anything heavy in it and stretches at the handle joins within a year. Eighteen ounce canvas is roughly twice the weight. It is stiff enough that the bag stands up on its own, which sounds like a small thing until you have tried to load groceries into a bag that keeps falling over on the counter.

The handles matter more than the cloth. Almost every tote that fails, fails where the handle meets the body. Ours are not stitched to the rim; they run all the way down the outside of both faces to the base seam, so a load pulls against the whole panel rather than against two inches of stitching. Each join is bar-tacked, which is a dense zig-zag repeated forty-two times in the same place. It is not elegant. It does not come apart.

What it will do over time

  • Month one: stiff, pale, slightly rough. It will not fold flat and it will hold a crease.
  • Month three: softened where it folds, darker where you hold the handles. It starts to slump when empty.
  • Year one: the base has taken a permanent shape. There will be marks. Coffee, ink and bicycle grease do not wash out of undyed cotton and we are not going to pretend they do.
  • Year five: considerably softer, noticeably darker at the handles, structurally identical. We have not yet had one returned as worn out.

What it is not

It is not waterproof and it is not water resistant. Undyed cotton canvas in rain will wet through in about ten minutes and dry in about two hours. It has no zip, no closure and no laptop padding. If any of those matter to you, this is the wrong bag, and we would rather say so on the product page than in a return email.
