Waxed Field Parka

348 USD. In stock in olive, sizes XS to XXL. A black run of 120 units is confirmed for 19 November 2026.

Gallery

Five frames, unretouched, shot in daylight in the studio and on the street outside it. The olive reads slightly warmer in direct sun than it does under cloud; both are in the set above.

Specification

  • Fabric: 100% organic cotton, 10 oz, dry-finish wax. Woven and waxed at a mill in Lancashire, England that has been doing it since 1864. Dry finish means the wax is worked into the fibre rather than sitting on the surface, so it does not transfer onto car seats.
  • Trims: Brass two-way YKK zip under a five-button storm flap. Corozo nut buttons. Cotton drill pocket bags. Cotton tape seam binding. No lining, so you can see and repair every seam.
  • Fit: Relaxed, cut to layer over a heavy knit. At size M: chest 48 in, waist 46 in, hem 50 in, body length 32 in from high point of shoulder, sleeve 25.5 in from shoulder seam.
  • Care: Do not machine wash. Do not dry clean. Do not use detergent of any kind. Sponge dirt off with cold water and let it dry away from heat. Re-wax once a season with any pure paraffin dressing.
  • Sizing: XS (US 0-2), S (US 4-6), M (US 8-10), L (US 12-14), XL (US 16-18), XXL (US 20-22). Unisex block. True to size over a knit; size down one for a closer cut over a shirt only.
  • Model: 5 ft 11 in with a 38 in chest, wearing size M over the fisherman rib sweater.
  • Weight: 1,420 g at size M.
  • Made in: Cut and sewn in Toronto, Ontario. Cloth woven in Lancashire, England.

What it is for

This is the jacket for a wet, cold, windy day where you are outdoors for hours rather than minutes. Waxed cotton blocks wind almost completely and sheds water for a long time without the clamminess of a plastic shell, because the cotton underneath still breathes. It is heavy. A parka at 1.4 kilos feels substantial in a way a synthetic shell does not, and some people dislike that. If you want something you forget you are wearing, buy the ripstop anorak instead.

It is cut long, to mid-thigh, so it covers a sweater properly and does not ride up when you reach. The hood adjusts at three points, including one at the back of the head, which is the adjustment that actually stops a hood from blowing off. There is no lining, deliberately: an unlined waxed jacket is lighter, dries faster inside, and can be repaired by anyone with a needle because every seam is visible.

Five internal button loops take the quilted liner jacket. Buttoned in, the pair is comfortable to about minus fifteen with a knit underneath, which covers most of a Toronto or Montreal winter and the shoulder seasons anywhere else.

What it is not for

  • Sustained heavy rain over several hours. Water resistant is not waterproof. Take the rain shell.
  • Anywhere you cannot hang it up. Waxed cotton picks up creases where you fold it and keeps them.
  • High-output activity. It does not breathe enough to run or ski in.
  • Anyone who wants a jacket to stay looking new. It will scuff at the elbows and lighten at the seams inside three months.

Choosing between two sizes

  • Wearing it over a shirt only: take the smaller size.
  • Wearing it over the fisherman rib or the liner jacket: take the larger.
  • Over 6 ft 2 in: take the larger for the sleeve length, not the chest.
  • Still unsure: order both, keep one, return the other free. That is cheaper for us than a bad first order. See returns.
