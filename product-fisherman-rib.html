Fisherman Rib Sweater

225 USD. In stock in undyed ecru, sizes XS to XXL.

Gallery

Undyed wool photographs slightly differently in every light. The colour is a natural ecru with visible variation between fleeces; it is not a flat white and it is not bleached.

Specification

  • Fabric: 100% British wool, undyed ecru, 5 gauge. The fleece is bought as a lot from a single Yorkshire spinner, so the shade varies a little between production runs and not at all within one.
  • Construction: Full fisherman rib, fully fashioned. Fully fashioned means each panel is knitted to shape and linked by hand rather than cut out of a knitted sheet, which is slower and does not shed yarn at the seams.
  • Fit: Straight through the body with a dropped shoulder. At size M: chest 44 in, body length 27 in from high point of shoulder, sleeve 21 in from shoulder seam, cuff 3.5 in.
  • Care: Hand wash cold with a wool detergent, or a certified wool cycle at 400 rpm or less. Dry flat, in shape, away from heat. Never hang it wet. Never tumble dry.
  • Sizing: XS (US 0-2) to XXL (US 20-22), unisex block. Runs one size large. Take your usual size for a roomy fit and one down for a standard one.
  • Model: 5 ft 8 in with a 34 in chest, wearing size S.
  • Weight: 760 g at size M.
  • Made in: Knitted and linked in Ontario, Canada. Yarn spun in West Yorkshire, England.

Why a fisherman rib

Fisherman rib is a variation on a two-by-two rib in which every other row is knitted into the row below. The effect is a much thicker, squashier fabric than a plain rib at the same yarn weight, with far more trapped air in it. Air is what actually keeps you warm; wool is only the structure that holds it still. That is why this sweater is warmer than a merino crewneck twice as dense, and why it weighs what it weighs.

The trade-off is that fisherman rib has almost no drape. It stands away from the body. If you want a sweater that follows your shape, buy the mock neck rib knit, which is a fine gauge and does exactly that. This one is designed to be a structural layer under a coat and it looks like one.

The wool is undyed for two reasons. The first is that dyeing wool takes roughly forty litres of water per garment and a good deal of heat. The second is that undyed wool keeps its natural lanolin content, which makes it slightly water repellent. A fisherman rib in undyed wool sheds a light rain long enough to get from a car to a door, which is exactly what fishermen wanted from it in the first place.

Honest drawbacks

  • It sheds a little for the first two wearings. Undyed rustic wool always does.
  • It is prickly at first against bare skin. Most people find it settles after three or four wearings; some never get on with it. Wear the waffle henley underneath if you are one of them.
  • It will pill under the arms and along the sides. All wool does. A comb fixes it, and one is included in the box.
  • It is 760 grams. That is heavy for a sweater.
  • It cannot go in a normal wash. If you will not hand wash things, the merino crewneck is machine washable and a better choice.

Choosing between two sizes

  • Wearing it alone, close to the body: take one size down from your usual.
  • Wearing it under the field parka: take your usual size.
  • You want the classic oversized fisherman look: take one up.
  • Between two sizes on the chest but long in the arm: take the larger and turn the cuff back once. The cuff is knitted deep for this reason.
