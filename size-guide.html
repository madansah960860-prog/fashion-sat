Size Guide

All measurements on this page are in inches, taken from the garment laid flat, not from a body. We use United States sizing, XS to XXL, with the US numeric equivalent printed beside it. Canadian numeric sizing is the same as United States numeric sizing for ready-to-wear, so a Canadian 8 and a US 8 are the same garment.

The single most useful thing you can do before ordering is measure a garment you already own and like, and compare that number against the tables below. It is far more reliable than measuring your body, because it accounts for how much room you actually want.

How to measure a garment you own

Lay it flat on a table, buttoned or zipped, and smooth it out without stretching. Use a tape measure or a ruler.

  1. Chest. Measure straight across from armpit seam to armpit seam. Double that number. That is the figure in our tables.
  2. Body length. From the highest point of the shoulder seam, next to the collar, straight down to the hem.
  3. Sleeve. From the shoulder seam to the end of the cuff. For dropped-shoulder knitwear, measure from where the seam actually falls, not from your shoulder.
  4. Shoulder. Across the back, from one shoulder seam to the other.
  5. Waist and hem. Straight across at the narrowest point and at the bottom edge. Double each.

How to measure yourself

If you would rather work from body measurements, stand relaxed and do not hold the tape tight.

  • Chest or bust. Around the fullest part, tape level all the way round, arms down.
  • Natural waist. Around the narrowest part of your torso, usually about an inch above the navel.
  • Hip. Around the fullest part, feet together.
  • Sleeve. From the bony point of your shoulder, down a slightly bent arm, to the wrist bone.
  • Height. Against a wall, without shoes. Useful for deciding between two sizes on body length.

Then add ease. Ease is the difference between your body and the garment. For a fitted knit, add 2 to 3 in to your chest measurement. For a relaxed knit or a jacket, add 6 to 8 in. For the field parka worn over a heavy sweater, add 10 to 12 in.

Tolerances, and what to do if something is off

Every measurement on this page has a tolerance of plus or minus half an inch. Cloth is cut by hand, knitwear relaxes at different rates depending on humidity, and anyone quoting garment measurements to a sixteenth of an inch is quoting a specification rather than a garment.

If something arrives outside that tolerance, email hello@plaintextsupply.ca with the order number and the measurement you took. We will replace it and pay postage both ways, and that is not counted as a return.

If you are between sizes

  • Order both. Keep one, return the other with the free prepaid label. That is genuinely cheaper for us than a return-and-reorder cycle, and it is why our returns policy is written the way it is.
  • Or write to us. Tell us your height, the chest measurement of a garment you already like, and which piece you are looking at. We will tell you which size to take, and if we are wrong we will pay for the exchange.
  • Or visit the studio at 214 Sterling Road, Toronto, Tuesday to Saturday, and try things on. Everything in the range is on the rail.
