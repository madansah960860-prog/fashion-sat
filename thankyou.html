Plaintext Supply Co.

We make one wardrobe. It does not change with the season, it is not restyled twice a year, and it is not photographed to look like something it is not. Fourteen core garments, made in runs we can actually finish, sold directly to you from a small studio on Sterling Road in Toronto.

This website is deliberately undesigned. There is no carousel, nothing slides in as you scroll, and no photograph has been colour-graded to make a grey coat look warmer than it is. What you can read here is the specification: the fibre content, the measured fit, the wash instructions, the height of the person wearing it in the picture, and the price. If a garment needs a mood board to be convincing, it is not a very good garment.

How this shop works

Plaintext Supply Co. is a direct-to-consumer clothing business. We are based in Toronto, we ship from Toronto, and we sell only through this website and through the small number of independent shops listed on our stockists page. There is no department store markup in these prices because there is no department store.

Prices and currency

Every price on this site is shown in United States dollars (USD), including for Canadian customers. We chose one currency and stuck to it so that a Vancouver shopper and a Chicago shopper are quoted the same number for the same coat. Your card issuer converts USD to Canadian dollars at its own rate on the day you order, and most Canadian banks add a foreign transaction fee of roughly 2.5 per cent. We would rather tell you that in advance than surprise you on your statement. Full detail is on the shipping and delivery page.

Shipping

  • Canada: flat 12 USD. Free on orders over 150 USD. Two to six business days from Toronto depending on province.
  • United States: flat 12 USD. Free on orders over 150 USD. Three to eight business days. Our goods qualify as North American origin under CUSMA, so there is no duty to pay on delivery.
  • Everywhere else: calculated at checkout. Import duty and local tax are the buyer's responsibility and we say so before you pay.

Canadian orders have GST, HST or GST plus PST added at checkout according to your province. The tax line is itemised on your receipt.

Returns

Thirty days from delivery, unworn, tags on. Canadian and United States customers get a prepaid return label and we absorb the cost. Exchanges for a different size ship free both ways, once per order. The exact process, with the address to send things to, is set out on the returns and exchanges page.

Gift cards and referrals

Gift cards are issued in USD in denominations of 50, 100, 200 and 500, never expire, and are not redeemable for cash. Our referral programme gives the person you refer 20 USD off their first order over 120 USD, and gives you 20 USD once that order passes the 30-day return window. We wait for the window to close so that nobody is credited for a return. Details on the FAQ page.

Why the site looks like this

People ask, so here is the answer. A clothing website is usually built to make you feel something before you have read anything. All of that costs money, and the cost ends up in the price of the coat. We wanted the opposite order: the specification first, then whatever atmosphere the photographs happen to carry on their own.

So the design here is doing a job rather than making an impression. One serif for headings and one sans for reading, both already on your device, so no font file downloads and nothing shifts as the page settles. One accent colour, taken from the rust in the waxed cotton, used only where it means something: a price, a link, the thing you are meant to press. Photographs are cropped to one ratio so a rail of twenty-six products can be compared rather than admired.

Nothing moves without a reason. Blocks settle in as you reach them, cards lift under the cursor, and every bit of it switches off completely if your device asks for reduced motion. There is no carousel, no pop-up, no interstitial, no auto-playing video, and no script watching you read.

The practical result is a page that opens on a slow connection in a rural township about as fast as it does on fibre in downtown Toronto, works at 320 pixels wide on an old iPhone, follows your system into dark mode, and can be read start to finish by a screen reader without anything getting in the way. We think that is a better use of the budget than a video header.
